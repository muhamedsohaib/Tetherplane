# DRAFT: Contributor Rights / Dual-Licensing Intake

> Internal planning only; NOT a contributor license agreement (CLA), copyright assignment or DCO. Nobody has been asked to sign anything.

## Ownership principle

An individual's GitHub commit, a DCO signoff, an automation-created commit or a public MIT/Apache contribution does **not by itself** prove who owns copyright or that a designated entity can relicense it under proprietary customer contracts. Employer or client assignments and provenance matter.

## Past-code review procedure

1. Inventory main, tags, branch-only commits, external patches, imported examples, generated artifacts and components distributed in v0.1.0.
2. Build a component-to-rights-holder table with evidence of original authorship and copyright/assignment status.
3. Identify third-party licenses and notice obligations; separate code that cannot safely join a proposed commercial grant.
4. Consult counsel about machine-generated components and contributors' employer/work-for-hire claims.
5. Preserve prior MIT/Apache permissions irrespective of future re-licensing.

## Future contribution workflow for counsel

- Choose whether to use a non-exclusive inbound CLA explicitly granting the project licensor enough rights for **AGPL-3.0-only publication AND separately negotiated commercial licensing**, or a formal assignment where appropriate.
- Verify actual holder consent, patent implications, rights to import third-party code, authority under employment arrangements, and record retention/privacy.
- A DCO may supplement provenance but must **not** be mistaken for a broad dual-license grant.
- Establish a process for declining or segregating contributions when rights cannot be cleared; do not fabricate retroactive consent.
- Do not activate contributor bot/forms until counsel approves the text and owner separately authorizes contributor communications.

## Draft required fields for a future lawyer-authored instrument

Party identification, scope of contributions, contributor authority, inbound copyright and patent rights, ability to make alternative commercial licenses, retained contributor use rights, warranty/indemnity treatment, attribution, governing law, acceptance method and verifiable record.

References: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/why-assign.html ; https://github.com/github/opensource.guide/blob/main/_articles/legal.md .
